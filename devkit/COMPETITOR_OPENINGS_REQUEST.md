# Data request: competitor openings near your branches

**Why we are asking, what we will do with it, and exactly what we need.**

Send the table at the bottom. Everything above it is context you can skip if
you already know why this matters.

---

## The short version

We can tell you which candidate sites are best. We cannot yet tell you what
one would earn, and there is a single missing number behind that.

Our catchment model asks how far people will travel past one shop to reach
another. That trade-off is governed by one exponent. Every published study
fits it from data; we have been assuming it. Across its plausible range, our
estimate of what a site is worth moves by about **1.4x** — so we can rank sites
for you, but we cannot price them.

**The cheapest way to measure it is something that has already happened to
you.** Every time a competitor opened near one of your branches, that branch's
sales moved. How much they moved, against how far away the new store was,
measures the exponent directly.

We already hold your sales history. We need you to tell us the openings.

---

## Why this beats the alternatives

We tried fitting the exponent from your five branches' revenue. It failed, and
the way it failed is worth stating plainly: the fit ran to the degenerate case
where distance does not matter at all. Five shops in one city do not differ
enough in competitive position to separate the effect of location from the
effects of size, format, catchment wealth and management.

Competitor openings solve that, because each one compares **a branch to
itself**. Everything that makes your Karen shop different from your Westlands
shop stays constant across the opening date and cancels out. That is why three
or four openings are worth more than five branches.

---

## What we need

For any branch that had a competitor open **within about 3 km** in roughly the
last three years:

| Your branch | Competitor that opened | Roughly where | Approx. opening date |
|---|---|---|---|
| | | | |

**On precision.** "Naivas opened on Ngong Road, around March 2024" is enough.
We will find the exact location ourselves from the mapping data. A month is
fine; a week is not needed.

**What counts as an opening.** A new shop, a competitor's major expansion or
refit, or a relocation into your area. A competitor **closing** near a branch
is just as useful — it is the same experiment run backwards, and those are
often easier to remember.

**How many.** Three or four are enough to be worth running. Six or more and we
can also tell you whether the effect differs by store format.

**What we do not need.** Nothing about your customers, no loyalty data, no
personal information of any kind. Only the shop locations and dates.

---

## Two questions worth answering in the same email

**1. Floor areas.** We currently assume most competitors are the same size,
because the open mapping data does not carry floor areas. Since a store's pull
is directly proportional to its size, this is the second-largest source of
error after the exponent. Rough bands are plenty:

> Naivas is usually about X sq ft, Quickmart about Y, Carrefour about Z.

**2. Your own branch sizes and coordinates.** We are currently working from
estimates for both. Real figures remove a whole class of doubt from every
number we give you, and they take ten minutes to write down.

---

## What you get back

Once we have the openings we can:

- **fit the exponent** instead of assuming it, and narrow every estimate we
  have given you;
- **quantify the threat** a new competitor poses to each existing branch,
  before it opens rather than after;
- move from *"these are the better sites"* to *"this site is worth
  approximately this much, give or take this much"*.

Until then every figure we publish carries its range, and we will keep saying
which conclusions survive the whole range and which do not.
