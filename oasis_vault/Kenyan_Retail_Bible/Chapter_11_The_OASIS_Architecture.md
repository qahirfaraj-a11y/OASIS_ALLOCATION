# Chapter 11: The OASIS Architecture (The Algorithmic Brain)

To the absolute beginner, an "Ordering System" is a digital notebook. You see a shelf is empty, you type "10 boxes" into the computer, and it emails the supplier. This is not a system; it is an electronic suicide note. It relies on human observation, which is slow, biased, and physically impossible at scale.
To the expert operator, the OASIS Engine is an autonomous financial pilot. It does not wait for a human to see an empty shelf. It uses the nodes.csv and edges.csv data to predict the future. OASIS is built on four core sub-routines, each managing a specific threat to your working capital.
11.1 Sub-Routine A: AMIT (Assortment & Margin Integration Tool)
The Role: The Gatekeeper.
AMIT is the firewall that prevents "Parasite" suppliers and "Cannibal" SKUs from re-infecting your store.
The Logic: Every time a Purchase Order (PO) is drafted, AMIT checks the Category Cap we established in Chapter 9. If a supplier tries to slip in a new 569th SKU into a department with a 500-SKU limit, AMIT automatically deletes the line item.
The Expert Edge: AMIT forces the One-In, One-Out rule. It identifies the SKU in that category with the lowest GMROI (Chapter 2) and mandates its removal before the new item can be ingested.
11.2 Sub-Routine B: LATA (Lead-Time & Allocation)
The Role: The Logistical Shield.
LATA manages the most dangerous variable in Kenyan retail: the Supplier Lead-Time (LT).
The Logic: A supplier may promise 2-day delivery, but your GRN logs (Chapter 4) show they actually take 5 days 40% of the time. LATA ignores the supplier's promise and uses the Historical Variance.
The Expert Edge: LATA calculates the Safety Stock (SS) dynamically. If a supplier is unreliable, LATA automatically inflates the Safety Stock to prevent a stock-out. If the supplier improves their delivery speed, LATA shrinks the Safety Stock to free up your working capital.
11.3 Sub-Routine C: DHARAM (Demand, Halo, and Revenue Analytics)
The Role: The Revenue Engine.
DHARAM is the brain that understands Basket Affinity (Chapter 8).
The Logic: It doesn't just look at what sold; it looks at what should have sold. If the BROOKSIDE 500ML (Anchor) sold out at 2:00 PM, DHARAM knows that your KINGSMIL BREAD (Attachment) sales will drop by 40% for the rest of the day.
The Expert Edge: DHARAM prevents "Ghost Demand." If bread sales are low because milk was out of stock, a beginner system would lower the bread order. DHARAM realizes the bread sales were artificially suppressed by the "Broken Halo" and maintains the correct order volume, preparing for the moment the milk returns.
11.4 Sub-Routine D: MANDE (Market, Network, and Distribution Efficiency)
The Role: The Negotiator.
MANDE evaluates your Supplier Efficiency Index (Chapter 2.5).
The Logic: Every quarter, MANDE runs a Network Triage. It identifies suppliers whose products have high substitution edges and low revenue.
The Expert Edge: MANDE generates the Purge Report. It tells the operator: "Supplier X is trapping 400,000 KES. If we delist them and move the volume to Supplier Y, our Net Capital Position improves by 12 days." MANDE provides the hard data you take to the negotiation table to weaponize your delisting power (Chapter 6).
