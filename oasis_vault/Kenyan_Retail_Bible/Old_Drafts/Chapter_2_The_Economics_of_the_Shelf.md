# Chapter 2: The Economics of the Shelf

*Unit economics, margin structures, and the working capital realities of the Kenyan market.*

## 💰 The Unit Economics of Survival
In Kenyan retail, profit is found in the decimals. With over **23,000 SKUs** in the global census, we see a stark divide between "Staple" economics and "Impulse" economics.

### 1. Margin Structures: The Staple Trap
*   **The Fresh Sector (Milk/Bread)**: High velocity but dangerously thin margins.
*   **Case Point**: Our census shows **Brookside 500ML** often operating at a **negative margin (-7.88%)** during promotions.
*   **The Neural Fix**: Using these "Loss Leaders" to pull customers toward high-margin neighbors (e.g., Spices, Chocolates).

### 2. Working Capital Realities
Retail in Kenya is a game of **Cash Flow vs. Credit**.
*   **The Distributor Mesh**: Managing the payment cycles of 500+ suppliers is the primary bottleneck for new retailers.
*   **Inventory Intake (GRN)**: The accuracy of the intake at the gate determines the success of the capital cycle.

## 📉 Why FMCG Models Fail
Traditional FMCG (Fast Moving Consumer Goods) models often fail in Kenya when they ignore the **Neural Connectivity** of the format. 
*   **The Mistake**: Flooding a Duka with slow-moving specialty items.
*   **The Strategy**: Only stock nodes with high "Substitution Density" and "Basket Affinity."

## 🔬 Deep-Dive: Margin vs. Velocity
| Strategy | Goal | Typical Department | Margin |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Velocity Driver** | Foot Traffic | Fresh Milk, Bread | 0% - 12% |
| **Margin Builder** | Profit Capture | Chocolate, Spices | 25% - 40% |
| **Systemic Anchor** | Loyalty | Sugar, Flour | 5% - 10% |

Understanding these numbers is the difference between a thriving store and a bankrupted one.
