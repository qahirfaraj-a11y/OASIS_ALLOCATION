# Chapter 8: Basket Affinity and The Halo Effect

*Which SKUs act as anchors? Mapping the mathematical probability of cross-category purchases.*

## 🥘 The Halo Effect
In the 23,000-node network, some nodes behave as "Gravity Anchors." Their presence in a customer's basket doesn't just represent a sale; it creates a "Halo" that drags other nodes into the transaction.

### 1. The Multiplier Effect
For every unit of an **Anchor SKU** (e.g., `BROOKSIDE 500ML`), there is a statistical probability of a secondary purchase:
*   **The Breakfast Cycle**: Milk (95% affinity) -> Bread (60% affinity) -> Sugar (20% affinity).
*   **The Evening Cycle**: Beer (70% affinity) -> Snacks (40% affinity) -> Cigarettes (30% affinity).

### 2. Identifying the Halo SKUs
*   **Primary Anchors**: These are high-velocity, low-margin nodes (The "Staple Trap"). They have the highest Halo Effect but the lowest direct profit.
*   **Secondary Profit Builders**: These are the nodes that the Halo "drags" in—items like `AJAB 2KG FLOUR` or `FESTIVE BREAD`. These are where the store's margin is actually built.

## 🕹️ Strategy: The Neural Placement
Strategic store layout (as discussed in Chapter 3) depends on this data. By separating the **Anchor SKU** (Milk) from the **Halo SKU** (Spread) with a high-margin "Impulse Node" (Chocolate), you maximize the transaction value.

## 🏁 Conclusion
The successful retailer doesn't sell "Milk." They sell the **Morning Routine**. By mastering the Halo Effect, you transition from "moving boxes" to managing the **Neural Flow of the Household**.
