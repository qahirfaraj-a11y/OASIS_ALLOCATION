# Chapter 6: Capital Flow and Supplier Dynamics

*Credit terms, cash flow cycles, and managing distributor relationships.*

## 💳 The Pulse of the Business
Retail is the art of turning cash into boxes and boxes back into cash—faster than your bills fall due. In the Kenyan landscape, this is complicated by a highly fragmented supply mesh.

### 1. Credit Terms: The Working Capital Bridge
*   **The Standard**: Most Tier 1 suppliers (Brookside, Coca-Cola) operate on strict 7 to 14-day credit cycles.
*   **The Opportunity**: Smaller, high-margin suppliers (e.g., KAPA Oil) may offer longer terms (30 days) to secure shelf nodes.
*   **Neural Strategy**: Use your "Velocity Tiers" to determine which suppliers deserve cash-on-delivery (high turnover) vs. those who must support your working capital through credit (slow movers).

### 2. Cash Flow Cycles
A store's liquidity is tied to its **GRN-to-Sales** interval.
*   **Negative Margin Nodes**: When you stock items like Brookside Milk at a loss leader price, you are "borrowing" foot traffic from your future profit. This must be offset by the high-margin clusters identified in the network.
*   **The Leakage Trap**: Discrepancies between the PO price and the GRN invoice price can cause "Invisible Leakage"—where your books show profit but your bank account is empty.

## 🤝 Managing Distributor Relationships
The Distributor is the "Middle Node" between the manufacturer and your gate.
*   **Compliance Monitoring**: Use the **Fill Rate** data from Chapter 4 to hold distributors accountable. A distributor with a <80% fill rate is effectively increasing your operational cost.
*   **Information Asymmetry**: By sharing your "Neural Insights" (e.g., "Your brand is losing to a substitute on my shelf"), you can negotiate better visibility discounts or marketing support.

## 🏁 Conclusion of Part II
We have mastered the "Ground Truth" of moving, receiving, and paying for stock. We are now ready to dive into the **23,000-Node Core**—the mathematical heart of the Kenyan Retail Bible.
