# Chapter 7: Mapping the SKU Universe (Executing the Vendor Purge)

To the absolute beginner, a supplier catalog of 1,000 items looks like an opportunity. They believe that if they put all 1,000 items on the shelf, they will attract 1,000 different types of customers.
To the expert operator looking at the neural network data, a massive catalog is a declaration of war on their working capital. The data proves that out of 23,000 SKUs in the Kenyan market, a staggering 84% are dormant. They do not generate foot traffic, they do not spin cash, and they only exist to cannibalize the sales of the 16% that actually work.
To protect your ecosystem, you must execute The Purge. You use the Tectonic Plate metrics (Capital Efficiency, Link Density, and Substitution Density) to classify every supplier into an operational kill-box.
7.1 The Untouchables (Protect and Expand)
Before you cut, you must know what to protect. These are the Elite Stabilizers. They possess mathematically flawless catalogs.
The Network Data: Look at Kenya Breweries Ltd (KBL) and Aquamist Limited. The data shows KBL generated nearly 14 Million KES using only 36 SKUs. Aquamist generated 13.5 Million KES using only 40 SKUs.
The Neural Signature: High Revenue, Low SKU Count, Zero Substitution Edges. A customer who wants a Tusker does not substitute it for a soda. A customer who wants a 20L Aquamist refill does not buy 40 half-liter bottles instead.
The Operational Treatment (VIP Logistics): You do not negotiate aggressively on margin with these suppliers, because their Capital Efficiency is already perfect. Instead, you treat them as VIPs. You give them premium end-caps. You prioritize their trucks at the GRN Checkpoint. If KBL wants to introduce a new SKU, you auto-approve it, because their historical network data proves they do not dump "Cannibal" products onto your floor.
7.2 The Necessary Evils (The Core vs. Tail Mandate)
This is where the surgery begins. These are the Apex Dictators—the massive traffic engines that you cannot live without, but who actively try to bloat your shelves.
The Network Data: Look at Brookside Dairy Limited. They generate massive foot traffic (over 37 Million KES), but they occupy 165 different SKUs to do it.
The Neural Signature: High Velocity on the "Core" (the 500ml pouch), but massive Capital Bleed on the "Tail" (the 250ml obscure flavored milks and premium yogurts that spin once a week).
The Operational Treatment (The Core Mandate): The beginner accepts all 165 SKUs because they are terrified of losing the 500ml pouch. The expert splits the supplier in half. You isolate the top 10 SKUs (The Black Holes) that drive the traffic. You accept standard terms for those. Then, you draw a hard line on the remaining 155 SKUs.
The Ultimatum: You tell the distributor: "I will take your 500ml pouches on 7-day credit. But I am delisting 80 of your slow-moving flavored milks. If you want them on my shelf, they must be supplied on 30-day Consignment. I am not financing your product experimentation." You use the power of their own Core products to force them to absorb the risk of their Tail products.
7.3 The Parasites (The Absolute Purge)
This is the kill-zone. These are the Portfolio Traps operating in the Cannibal Zones ([[WINES]], [[SPICES]], [[COSMETICS]]).
The Network Data: Look at Twirl Enterprises or the massive wine importers. The network shows an importer holding 500+ SKUs, but generating less than 300,000 KES in total monthly revenue.
The Neural Signature: Lowest Capital Efficiency (< 500 KES per SKU), and Maximum Substitution Edges. A mid-tier South African Merlot from Supplier A has 400 direct substitution links to a mid-tier South African Merlot from Supplier B.
The Operational Treatment (The Guillotine): You do not negotiate with a Parasite; you execute a mathematically precise purge.
Identify the Predator SKUs: You query the network to find the top 20% of the wines that actually spin cash. These are the Predators.
Execute the Prey: You immediately delist the bottom 80% (the Prey). You box them up, send them back to the supplier, and demand a credit note.
The Financial Result: Because the network proves these items possess massive substitution edges, your total revenue does not drop. The customer walks in, sees their obscure wine is gone, and simply buys the Predator wine you left on the shelf. You maintain 100% of the category revenue, but you have instantly freed up 80% of the working capital previously trapped in that aisle.
7.4 The Algorithmic Guardrails (Preventing Re-Infection)
A successful purge creates empty shelf space. Empty shelf space is a vacuum, and FMCG distributor reps are trained to fill vacuums. If you do not lock the system down, the Parasites will re-infect your store within 30 days.
To prevent this, the expert operator implements a strict One-In, One-Out Policy hardcoded into their procurement algorithm.
If a spice supplier wants to introduce a new Turmeric powder to your [[SPICES]] department (which is a strict Cannibal Zone), they are legally forbidden from just adding it to the catalog.
To get the new Turmeric listed, the supplier must voluntarily select an existing, slow-moving SKU from their own portfolio to be permanently delisted and removed from the store.
The physical footprint of a Cannibal Zone is locked. The SKUs must fight to the death for the existing square footage.

This marks the transition from physical space management to absolute data supremacy. By executing this purge, you shift the store from a bloated warehouse into a lean, high-velocity cash engine.
