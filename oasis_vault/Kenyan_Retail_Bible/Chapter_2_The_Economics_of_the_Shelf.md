# Chapter 2: The Economics of the Shelf (Why Variety Kills)

The fastest way to spot a beginner in retail is to listen to how they talk about profit. A beginner talks about Gross Margin %. An expert talks about Velocity.
2.1 The Myth of the Flat Margin
If a supplier offers you a bottle of imported lotion with a 35% profit margin, and a loaf of everyday bread with a 10% profit margin, the beginner assumes the lotion is the better product. This is a fatal error. In retail, you do not pay your rent with percentages; you pay it with physical cash.
2.2 The GMROI Equation (The Ground Truth)
Experts measure success using GMROI (Gross Margin Return on Inventory Investment). This measures how many times a single product spins your cash in a 30-day cycle.
If you invest 10,000 KES into the 35% margin lotion, it sits on your shelf for 30 days. You make 3,500 KES, but your initial 10,000 KES was trapped for a full month.
If you invest 10,000 KES into the 10% margin bread, it sells out in one day. You make 1,000 KES. But because you have your 10,000 KES back, you buy more bread the next day. By the end of the month, that same 10,000 KES has spun 30 times, generating 30,000 KES in pure cash profit.
The fast, low-margin item is mathematically superior to the slow, high-margin item.
2.3 The Supplier-Funded Float
As a store grows from a Duka to a Mega-Store, it adds variety. Variety slows down Velocity (because specialty items don't sell every day). So how do Mega-Stores make money if their GMROI drops?
They stop using their own money. Because Mega-Stores move massive volumes of items like Coca-Cola and Brookside, they force suppliers to give them 30-day to 45-day credit terms.
The store receives the stock on Day 1, sells it all to the customer by Day 10, and holds the physical cash in their bank account for 20 days before they have to pay the supplier. They use this "free" supplier money to pay their staff, pay their rent, and fund their business expansion. A scaled retail store is actually a highly efficient, short-term shadow bank.
